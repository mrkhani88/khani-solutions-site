Transient wake: does the circulation persist?

The 0°–20° screening sweep below compares separate steady fields. This follow-up holds the reconstructed aircraft at 20° and 100 mph and advances the flow in physical time. It asks whether the apparent circulation survives startup, changes with time and accompanies fluctuating aerodynamic loads.

The finding

Opposite-sign circulation persists on both sides of the modeled wake throughout the sampled interval, while lift, drag and side force fluctuate. The calculation supports sustained, unsteady circulation in this model. A unique periodic shedding frequency and the real aircraft’s stall behavior remain unestablished.

Loads and circulation: the measured evidence

Statistics exclude the first 0.5 s provisionally and use approximately 1.5 s of subsequent flow evolution. Lift fluctuation RMS is 275.5 N, or 1.05% of mean lift; drag fluctuation RMS is 79.3 N, or 0.92% of mean drag. RMS here means the standard deviation about the time-weighted mean, not the RMS of the total force. The L/D value is the ratio of the two mean forces. Full-domain force integrals are used directly, without doubling.

WAKE_FORCE_TABLE

Circulation is computed independently of the vortex rendering as Γ = ∮ U · dl around four fixed, closed, radius-1 m circles normal to the freestream. Their centers use X = 6 and 10 m, Y = ±6.8 m and Z = 2.65 + (X + 2.2) tan(20°) m in the model coordinate system. Each circle has 128 segments; every sampled point passed the native-field validity check. The loop orientation is +span followed by +lift.

WAKE_GAMMA_TABLE

The circulation sign never reverses in any of the four sampled loops: near-station means are approximately −23.21 and +23.22 m²/s; farther-station means are −21.53 and +21.55 m²/s. This is evidence of persistent opposite-sense circulation through these particular loops. The smaller magnitude at the farther loops alone does not establish vortex decay: fixed loops can capture different fractions of a wandering or spreading structure.

Three consecutive window means vary by less than 0.6% of mean lift and 0.4% of mean drag. This is an encouraging consistency check, not proof of stationary statistics. A detrended, uniformly resampled Welch spectrum has a lift/drag peak candidate near 6.67 Hz with approximately 1.33 Hz bin spacing. The averaging interval contains only about ten cycles of that candidate. It is not reported as a validated shedding frequency; repeated agreement in wake probes, longer sampling and timestep sensitivity are still required.

How to read the video

  • Aircraft colors: signed local lift traction in Pa, obtained by projecting pressure and viscous surface loading onto the freestream-normal lift direction. Positive and negative colors show contributions to lift; they are not pressure coefficient, structural stress or a local lift coefficient.
  • Cyan lines and arrowheads: instantaneous streamlines traced from each native velocity field, with seeds around the wings and body and selection for deflection. They show the local flow direction at that instant. They are not particle trajectories across multiple times.
  • Translucent purple-to-yellow surfaces: Q = 500 s⁻² isosurfaces colored by local speed, 0–65 m/s. Q is a velocity-gradient diagnostic used to identify rotation-dominated regions; the colors encode speed, not circulation magnitude.
  • Right-hand plots: native lift and drag against physical time. The vertical cursor identifies the field currently displayed. Camera and aircraft remain stationary, so changes in flow structures come from the calculated time sequence.

The 1920 × 1080, 24 fps movie uses 75 native snapshots from 0.502 to 1.982 s, at 0.02 s spacing, displayed over 7.5 s. Playback is slowed by approximately five times. No synthetic shedding, reversed time sequence or interpolated flow was added. Mesh, numerical settings and execution evidence

Full-domain mesh and initialization

Native OpenFOAM mirrorMesh reflected the retained 2,226,297-cell half-aircraft mesh about Y = 0, producing 4,452,594 cells with no center-plane symmetry boundary. Mirroring increases the domain extent, not the resolution. The generic reconstructed exterior, absent propeller blades and no-prism-layer wall-function treatment remain as documented in the steady study below. Configured full-domain mesh checks passed; this does not remove the source mesh’s concavity concerns or establish near-wall adequacy.

The 20° steady iteration-1000 field provided the initial condition. Scalars were mirrored and the lateral velocity component reflected; the mapped cell-center coordinate discrepancy was below 5.7 × 10⁻¹⁴ m. A localized initial lateral velocity perturbation of up to 0.1% of freestream seeded asymmetric evolution. There was no continuing forcing. Because the initial steady field was unconverged, the 0.5 s startup exclusion remains provisional.

What this changes for design investigation

A steady image can suggest recirculation but cannot show its persistence or load variability. This transient result adds physical-time evidence: circulation remains measurable after the provisional startup interval, and the loads fluctuate even at a fixed operating point. That makes wing/body/boom junctions, wake interaction with the tail and sensitivity to geometric fairings useful candidates for controlled comparisons. The present run does not isolate which feature causes each fluctuation or demonstrate an improved design.

Scope: this is an independent, unpowered public-reference Archer Midnight approximation, displayed alongside Wisk-related methods demonstrations. It is not Wisk, Boeing or Archer engineering data. No rotor thrust or propwash, trim, ground effect, aeroelasticity, resolved LES turbulence, grid/time-step independence, flight-test correlation or validated stall threshold is included. About 1.5 s of sampled evolution is insufficient to declare a statistically converged wake or a reliable shedding frequency.

Rendering limit: VTK reported 27,309 non-manifold contour warnings over the sequence. Some concave native cells could not be contoured, so Q surfaces may have omissions. Their displayed area is not a complete physical vortex metric. Native forces, probe histories and fixed-loop circulation remain separate evidence; the interpretation above does not rely on a complete Q surface.
