Build the coupled model, step by step

1 · Keep the battery experiment, add explicit electronics loads

The battery retains the Stage 02 spatial example’s 2 W total: 80% in the assumed 12 × 18 mm patch and 20% distributed throughout the cell. All parts start at 25 °C. Sources run for 600 seconds, then turn off until 1,800 seconds. Prescribed heat generation is distinct from electrical input power.

The new electronics load is 1.75 W total, distributed as below. These are 25% of the original CAD’s illustrative package powers, chosen as a moderate teaching case; neither set is measured product data.

3 · Add heat paths between parts

Q̇i→j = Gij (Ti − Tj)   ·   Gcontact = A / R″total

A contact loses exactly as much heat from one node as it adds to the other. The resistance includes the participating half-layer thicknesses, interface material and an assumed area-specific contact resistance of 0.0002 m²·K/W for the solid contacts.

Internal air uses k = 0.026 W/(m·K), plus a fixed 4 W/(m²·K) radiative conductance between facing parts. Gap heights follow the simplified CAD: cell 0.8 mm, RF board 2.2 mm, main board 3.4 mm and exposed midplate 5.4 mm to the back cover; package tops use their remaining clearance. This is a prescribed gap model, without a fluid-flow calculation.

4 · Cool the outside of the product

Only the display’s exterior face, back cover’s exterior face and exposed frame exchange heat with the 25 °C room, using the assumed combined effective coefficient of 8 W/(m²·K). The buried battery and boards have no direct ambient sink. Internal radiation transfers heat between parts and is separate from this exterior loss term.

This changes the thermal paths, storage and boundary conditions from Stage 02. Comparing their absolute temperatures is therefore a model-development comparison, not a one-variable cooling experiment. The original Stage 01 and Stage 02 results remain available.

5 · Separate the sources to explain the result

CaseBattery sourceElectronics sourceCell mean at 10 min
Battery only2 W0 W35.38 °C
Electronics only0 W1.75 W30.67 °C
Both2 W1.75 W41.04 °C

The unpowered cell reaches 30.67 °C because the electronics warm the boards, midplate and back cover; those parts transfer heat into the cell. In the combined case, the cell’s own heating and the warmer surroundings both raise its temperature. With constant properties, the rises above ambient add: (35.38 − 25) + (30.67 − 25) ≈ 41.04 − 25 K, allowing for rounded numbers.

The RF board reaches a 64.70 °C local maximum, while the main PCB reaches 51.41 °C. The small RF board has concentrated sources and restricted paths to the enclosure. U07 reaches a 68.52 °C effective package temperature despite generating only 0.125 W. Temperature depends on neighboring sources and thermal resistance as well as a component’s own power. These values identify a bottleneck in this assumed network, not a device temperature limit or a qualified design.

After heat-off, some unpowered regions can keep warming briefly while hotter neighbors cool. Total product energy still decreases. A single time constant no longer describes the coupled system.

6 · Verify the equations before interpreting the maps

Ci dTi/dt = Q̇i − Σ Gij(Ti − Tj) − Gambient,i(Ti − 25 °C)

Conservative finite volumes handle lateral conduction. A symmetric conductance network joins layers and packages. Backward Euler advances the transient with a 2 s time step; SciPy factors the sparse matrix once and reuses it for all three sources. The browser displays saved results every 30 s; it does not run a new thermal solve.

  • Energy balance: supplied heat equals stored heat plus exterior losses to within 1.1 × 10⁻⁹ J over the run.
  • Source separation: combined temperature rise matches the sum of the two isolated-source rises to within 1.3 × 10⁻¹³ K before float32 browser export.
  • Spatial refinement: 9,683 versus 37,156 total nodes; largest change in a component’s maximum at 600 s is 0.308 K, at U07.
  • Time refinement: reducing 2 s to 1 s changes component maxima at 600 s by at most 0.0081 K.
  • Independent benchmark: a two-node network is compared with its exact matrix-exponential solution; error is below 0.000034 K with a 0.001 s step.

These checks support numerical consistency. They do not validate the assumed materials, gap model or contacts. Measurements of package/cell temperatures, thermal capacities, interface resistance and external conditions are still needed before product predictions. Stage 04 now tests an RF-to-enclosure thermal pad with matched before/after maps and fixed heat loads.

Included, collapsed and omitted geometry

Four enclosure solids, two boards, the cell and seven chip packages have temperatures. Adhesive, mounts, pedestal, TIM and RF posts are massless conductance links without independent temperatures. BMS parts, connectors, passives, camera, speaker and haptic are omitted from the thermal network. The optional upper SoC TIM / graphite / copper bridge is not active in this stage. Gray context may therefore include a collapsed link or omitted part.

The frame is a planar ring with its depth included in heat capacity and lateral conductance; its small USB cutout is omitted from the thermal mesh. The full CAD retains the opening. Accessory volumes do not displace the assumed cavity transfer paths. No through-thickness cell gradient, natural-convection flow, electrochemistry, temperature-dependent properties, deformation, safety assessment or thermal-runaway behavior is solved.
