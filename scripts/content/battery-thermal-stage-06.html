Why the temperatures change, step by step

1 · Freeze the Stage 05 stack and loading

Every case uses the same 0.10 mm graphite sheet (kxy = 800, kz = 5 W/(m·K)), 0.025 mm adhesive (k = 0.2 W/(m·K)), 2.075 mm RF pad and fixed exterior enclosure. The lower-right sheet corner stays at (68, 30) mm. Increasing width moves the left edge toward the battery; increasing height moves the upper edge toward its prescribed heat patch. This is a controlled size-and-coverage study: the chosen growth direction matters, so area alone cannot predict the result for other placements.

Initial and ambient temperature remain 25 °C. The battery generates 2 W and seven packages generate 1.75 W for 600 s, followed by 1,200 s with sources off. The same effective exterior loss coefficient, 8 W/(m²·K), acts only on display, back glass and frame. No buried layer receives a new direct ambient sink. The medium-size field is checked byte for byte against Stage 05.

2 · Change the footprint and count what comes with it

The sheet areas are 720, 1,500 and 3,500 mm². Their projected overlap with the battery is 0, 200 and 1,680 mm². Projected overlap means coupling across the modeled internal gap, not physical sheet-to-cell contact. The largest sheet reaches most of the prescribed cell heat patch at x = 27–39, y = 84–102 mm. Its upper edge ends at y = 100 mm.

Thickness and material properties stay fixed, so larger sheets necessarily add graphite mass, adhesive mass, thermal capacity and bonded area. They also redirect a larger share of internal gap paths from the back glass to the sheet. Those are physical consequences of this footprint change, not an isolated lateral-conductivity test; Stage 05 retains that separate control. Native FreeCAD and STEP checks confirm 67 valid, non-overlapping solids per size, 80 mm² of pad contact and bonded-sheet contact equal to each footprint area.

4 · Follow the spreading and the changed heat paths

Gx = kxt Δy/Δx   ·   Csheet = ρcpAt   ·   Q̇ij = Gij(Ti − Tj)

The sheet conductance per square, kxyt = 0.08 W/K, is unchanged. More area supplies additional lateral paths and connects the RF-pad region to more of the cover and cell surroundings. The small sheet ends below the cell’s prescribed heat patch; the large sheet reaches that region. Local heat flow reverses where a hotter part faces a cooler sheet. The battery mean reflects the whole cell’s energy, while its maximum tracks its hottest control volume. Neither should be inferred from the other.

Why can the battery average rise while its peak falls? The independent cell-energy audit finds that the large sheet removes an additional 64.05 J through the graphite path and 16.13 J through the cell-to-midplate path over the first 600 s. But its larger footprint replaces direct cell-to-cover gap paths that had removed 81.82 J more in the reference. The combined change leaves 1.647 J more stored in the cell. Dividing by its unchanged 29.98944 J/K capacity gives the modeled +0.0549 K average rise. Locally, the sheet reaches the prescribed hot patch and lowers the peak, while other cell regions warm. The positive net cell-to-sheet flow at 600 s alone cannot explain the full temperature history.

A spreader redistributes energy and adds transient heat capacity. Its benefit depends on access to cooler regions and on the exterior heat-removal path. The 40 and 45 °C thresholds are map diagnostics, not safety or touch limits. Areas count whole finite-volume cells by their center temperatures; no interpolated contour boundary is implied.

The table compares the same instant at the end of heating, not a steady state. Cooling continues after source shutoff and internal components can exchange stored heat. Use the timeline to inspect each saved time; full-precision component histories are available below.

6 · Test the numerical result before drawing a design conclusion

Every size is solved on a 2 mm grid with a 2 s backward-Euler step, then checked on a 1 mm grid and with a 1 s time step. Node counts are 10,070, 10,460 and 11,460; finer counts are 38,676, 40,236 and 44,236. The largest component-maximum grid difference at 600 s is 0.3447 K; the largest time-step difference is 0.00793 K. Maximum full-transient energy residual is 9.04e-10 J; isolated-source rises sum to the combined rise within 9.24e-14 K.

These paired checks are especially important for small battery differences: compare the change itself across meshes rather than treating an unsigned mesh error as a hardware confidence interval. This stage identifies a useful footprint tradeoff in the assumed model; it does not establish an optimal product design or a battery safety margin.

Limits inherited from Stage 05: one temperature per layer control volume through thickness, lumped packages, constant properties, resistance-based internal air conduction and fixed linearized radiation, no resolved airflow, no nonlinear radiation, compression mechanics, electrochemistry or measured contact properties. Sheet and adhesive have finite heat capacity and no ambient sink. Experimental material/interface data and independent tests are still required for physical validation.
