Why the temperatures change, step by step

1 · Preserve the largest-sheet reference

The graphite stays at x = 18–68 mm, y = 30–100 mm, with 0.10 mm thickness, kxy = 800 and kz = 5 W/(m·K). The 25 µm case reproduces the entire Stage 06 largest-sheet field byte for byte. Initial and ambient temperature remain 25 °C. Battery heat is 2 W and package heat totals 1.75 W for 600 s, followed by 1,200 s with sources off. Exterior loss remains 8 W/(m²·K) on display, cover and frame only.

2 · Fit three bondlines inside the fixed enclosure

The inner back cover stays at z = 7 mm. Adhesive grows inward, moving the graphite inward and shortening the installed RF pad from 2.075 to 2.050 to 2.000 mm. Pad contact stays 80 mm²; each bonded sheet interface stays 3,500 mm². The graphite-to-cell gap falls from 0.675 to 0.650 to 0.600 mm. The sheet remains separated from the cell; projected coupling is not physical contact.

Adhesive conductivity stays 0.2 W/(m·K), density 1,200 kg/m³ and specific heat 1,500 J/(kg·K). Each bonded contact retains R″ = 0.0001 m²·K/W. Pad and graphite properties remain unchanged. Thickness also changes the adhesive and pad lateral conductances. Adhesive sheet conductance rises from 0.000005 to 0.000020 W/K per square, still much smaller than graphite’s 0.08 W/K per square. This is a fitted-stack thickness study: it changes adhesive mass and vertical position as well as adhesive resistance. No compression or manufacturing tolerance is modeled.

The displayed bond resistance excludes graphite and cover half-layer terms. The actual graphite-center to cover-center path includes those terms: 0.000735 m²·K/W at 25 µm and 0.001110 at 100 µm. Bulk adhesive resistance increases fourfold, while the full center-to-center path rises by about 51% because the contact and half-layer terms remain. The larger adhesive resistance does not act alone: the adhesive gains 0.4725 J/K of capacity, while the shorter pad loses 0.0132 J/K; net assembly capacity rises by 0.4593 J/K.

4 · Isolate adhesive resistance from the fitted-stack changes

A separate diagnostic retains the 25 µm geometry, mass, pad, gaps, heat sources and all lateral conductances. It changes only the two adhesive half-thickness terms in the graphite-to-adhesive and adhesive-to-cover vertical resistances to the 100 µm equivalent. No extra material is installed in this numerical diagnostic. It is not another physical CAD design.

This comparison distinguishes the response to extra vertical resistance from the combined effects of added adhesive storage, shorter pad and smaller internal gaps. Resistance-only and fitted-stack results should not be treated as interchangeable. Their difference also includes coupling between those changes; it is not a separate measurement of heat-capacity effects.

In this run, resistance alone changes the battery average by +0.005886 K and its peak by +0.006288 K. The fitted 100 µm stack instead lowers both at 600 s. Thus the geometry and storage changes collectively reverse the resistance-only battery trend. A shorter cell-to-sheet gap strengthens coupling, while the added adhesive capacity changes the transient energy storage. Their individual contributions have not been isolated in this stage.

The graphite average rises from 39.02864 to 39.10036 °C even as the cover mean and cell average cool slightly. The cover maximum is near the frame edge outside the sheet, so a larger local bond resistance need not increase the global cover peak.

The cell receives 1,200 J. Its three outward heat paths plus stored energy sum to that input. Relative to 25 µm, the 100 µm fitted stack changes cell storage by -1.478843 J. Dividing by the unchanged cell capacity, 29.98944 J/K, gives -0.049312 K, matching the battery-average change. This audit uses the exported conductance networks and solver-consistent end-step integration.

For the 100 µm fitted stack, the graphite path removes 3.86047 J more from the battery than the reference. The midplate path removes 2.38998 J less and the uncovered-cover path removes 0.00835 J more. Together they remove an additional 1.47884 J, explaining the lower cell average at 600 s. This is a path balance for the full fitted design, not an attribution of each geometry or capacity change in isolation.

At 1,800 s, the battery average is 26.77642 °C for 25 µm and 26.78224 °C for 100 µm: the thicker fitted stack is now slightly warmer. These are transient comparisons, not steady-state results. Added thermal capacity can delay warming during the pulse and alter cooling afterward. The diagnostic tests added resistance separately; the full stack response includes resistance, storage and geometry effects.

6 · Check that the changes survive numerical refinement

Every physical case has 11,460 thermal nodes, including seven lumped packages; the 1 mm mesh has 44,236 nodes. Working time step is 2 s, checked at 1 s. Across physical cases, the largest component-maximum mesh difference at 600 s is 0.19737 K and time-step difference is 0.00790 K. The largest full-transient energy residual is 7.98e-10 J. Source superposition is also checked. The resistance-only diagnostic receives its own mesh/time and energy checks.

Limits: assumed properties and contacts, fixed linearized internal radiation, resistance-based air gaps without CFD flow, one temperature per layer cell through thickness, lumped packages, no electrochemistry, compression, nonlinear radiation or measured thermal validation. Gray CAD accessories and optional cooling-path candidates do not automatically add heat paths; only the named solved components and stated links participate. This stage is not a hardware adhesive recommendation or a battery safety margin.

Reproduce and inspect the adhesive comparison
