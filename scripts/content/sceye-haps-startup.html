Stage 05 / Cold-soaked startup · sensor and heater dynamics

Cold start, realistic response models and a reserved heater margin.

The Stage 04 controller worked from a warm initial motor. Starting its winding, housing, drive electronics and bracket at −40°C exposes the warm-up energy and power limits. The delayed case reaches the −5±0.5°C band in 153.7 minutes, but spends 2.61 hours near the 300 W hardware limit. A deliberate 270 W command ceiling retains 30 W of hardware reserve and reaches the band in 180.4 minutes.

02 / Follow the energy and feedback step by step

Why a cold start asks for more power.

Housing temperature→Sensor lag→PI + command ceiling→Command dead time→Delivered-power response→Housing heat + DC-bus demand
  1. Warm the thermal mass. The four motor-assembly capacities total 59 kJ/K. Raising all four from −40 to −5°C would store about 2.065 MJ (0.574 kWh), before accounting for concurrent motor losses, external losses and heat exchanged with the surrounding vehicle. The heater acts on the housing, so the four parts do not warm uniformly.
  2. Test the existing controller honestly. At startup the 35 K error requests 10.5 kW through its proportional term. The installed heater can deliver only 300 W. Anti-windup manages the integrator; it cannot supply unavailable heat. The ideal cold case and delayed cold case both spend about 2.6 h at the hardware limit.
  3. Add the feedback and output dynamics. Sensor temperature follows the housing gradually; the command arrives late and delivered power ramps. Their nominal few-second effect is small compared with an hours-long warm-up, but long sensor/output lags reduce damping around the target.
  4. Trade warm-up time for reserve. The 270 W ceiling keeps actual power 30 W below installed capacity and increases acquisition time by 26.7 min. Its slightly lower first-day heat use reflects spending longer cold, with smaller heat loss; it is not evidence of a more efficient heater.
  5. Retune the combined slow-response case. With the original gains, day 08 reaches 299.8 W and the maximum heater-on true-temperature error is 0.528 K, outside the chosen band. Reducing gains damps the night oscillation; Ki=0.5 leaves a larger warm-transition error. The Kp=200 / Ki=0.75 candidate brings the day 08 peak to 240.5 W and heater-on maximum error to 0.428 K. Inspect both the startup and settled windows before selecting a controller.
  6. Account for the whole system. Delivered heater power enters the motor housing and the common DC demand. Changed converter/battery losses feed back into the integrated 24-node thermal network. Cold battery charging permission remains active in the entire-network sensitivity, but prescribed hardware loads are not proven operable at −40°C.

05 / Numerical evidence and remaining physical tests

Check the coupled model before interpreting performance.

  • All 9 native mission cases plus a managed-startup refinement completed8 continuous days with finite data, bounded SOC, thermal/whole-system energy checks and final-cycle repeatability.
  • Warm-start reference reproduces the saved Stage04 selected PI across all 24 node temperatures within 0.000732K.
  • Maximum integration step2s / tolerance1e−7 versus0.5s /1e−8 changes exported node temperatures by at most 0.000177K; source/refinement comparisons are downloadable.
  • Separate analytical fixtures verify a 300 W step after2 s dead time with5 s first-order response, and a 30 s sensor response. Maximum analytical errors: 6.35e-10W and 1.71e-09K.
  • Thermal capacities, material paths, surfaces, native connections and mission electrical loss maps remain unchanged. Only initial motor/network temperatures and the explicitly listed control/environment overrides vary.

Next physical-data gate: measure mounted sensor/heater response, determine heater electrical limits versus bus voltage and cold resistance, select motor/ESC/battery allowables, add temperature-dependent loss maps and cold-start permissives, then correlate warm-up and cycling tests. Sensor bias/noise/failure, heater fail-on/off, transport jitter, PWM and spatial heater/contact hot spots remain unresolved. The assumed 59kJ/K motor assembly should be replaced by a mass/material heat-capacity budget.
