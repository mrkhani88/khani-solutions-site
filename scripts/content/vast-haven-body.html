03 / Inputs before results

What is known, and what we assumed

Coupling boundary: the orbital model, 80 W solid panel and 2 W air-bay model are separate first-round studies. Their loads, temperatures and fields have not yet been reconciled into one common vehicle heat budget. The solid temperature field does feed its own thermoelastic solve.

Public vehicle anchors

Vast describes a 10.1 m by 4.4 m station, four crew members, 80 m³ pressurized volume and 45 m³ habitable volume, with solar power, avionics cold plates, a thermal pump tray, batteries and life support.

Representative study inputs

Orbit timing, attitude, radiator area, heat capacities, power profiles, interface conductance, coolant properties and component targets are explicitly assigned engineering assumptions. Published power capability is not assumed to equal continuous internal waste heat.

The visual cabin volume has not been reconciled with the public 80 m³ value. CFD uses a separate local bay, not the full reconstructed pressure vessel.

How to explain this analysis
  1. Define dissipation and operating modes independently from solar-array rating.
  2. Route heat through component interfaces to cold plates and coolant.
  3. Reject heat through a radiator with absorbed environmental flux and emitted radiation.
  4. Carry thermal inertia and electrical energy through sunlight and eclipse.
  5. Compare the same mission with one declared design change, then check numerical energy closure.

05 / Component thermal and thermoelastic response

Heat spreading and mounting restraint

The local model resolves solid temperature gradients and maps the temperature field into a linear-elastic mount analysis. It uses separate, simplified geometry and assumed material properties. It does not establish the station’s pressure-vessel strength, launch loads or fatigue life.

Thermal-to-structural workflow and next checks
  1. Assign a component heat load, cooling boundary and contact resistance.
  2. Solve the 3D solid conduction model and inspect mesh sensitivity.
  3. Apply thermal strain relative to a stated reference temperature.
  4. Compare declared mounting restraints and examine displacement and stress away from constraint singularities.
  5. Resolve the real bracket, fasteners, contact, preload and allowable stresses before any hardware decision.

06 / Pressurized internal-flow CFD

Cabin airflow is a different cooling problem

Forced ventilation carries heat inside the pressurized habitat. The representative bay uses microgravity with no buoyancy. Outside the vehicle, orbital vacuum heat rejection requires radiation and conduction modeling.

Baseline mesh comparison

What this CFD does and does not show

Velocity and pressure come from the declared OpenFOAM mesh and boundary conditions. Any air-temperature field is a separately identified energy solution. The local-domain result is not a validated full-cabin comfort, contaminant-removal or life-support design. Inlet placement, actual obstructions, fan curves, acoustic constraints and cabin pressure require further definition.

What follows this first round

Measured correlation

Characterize heat loads, contact conductance, coating optical properties and coolant/fan behavior. Define thermal-balance and thermal-cycle test cases, calibrated sensor locations, uncertainty and independent validation runs. TVAC applies to vacuum-exposed hardware; a pressurized cabin needs its own controlled air and coolant test boundaries.

Design refinement

Investigate radiator orientation and view factors, coating degradation, pump-out and heater faults, local board detail, real fastener/contact behavior, and fan/inlet placement. These remain follow-on tasks rather than completed or validated results.
