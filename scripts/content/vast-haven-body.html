Stage 03 / Connected subsystem and mission study

One heat budget, from cold-plate CFD to the station model

The 600 W representative avionics assembly now shares a heat budget across OpenFOAM coolant-channel CFD, FreeCAD/CalculiX solid thermal and thermoelastic analysis, and the integrated OpenModelica mission model. Step 1: establish the shared design basis

Representative 24-hour transient, 60 minutes sunlight and 30 minutes eclipse, starting at assumed sunrise. Avionics 600 W, payload 800 W and crew-equivalent heat 480 W. Bulk upper limits: avionics 55 °C, battery 35 °C, payload 40 °C and cabin 27 °C. These are study assumptions, not Vast requirements.

Step 2: resolve the local thermal and fluid paths

A 300 × 200 × 6 mm spreader receives 600 W. Twelve identical 300 × 8 × 2 mm water channels receive 50 W each. Improving the interface at the same flow reduces the spreader peak from 91.28 to 79.11 °C; raising branch flow from 1 to 2 L/min then lowers it to 71.32 °C, at a 20 °C inlet. The PCB/device CAD solids provide packaging context; this is not a detailed chip-junction or ECAD model.

Step 3: check assembly expansion

For the same improved temperature field, permitting thermal expansion lowers the 95th-percentile extrapolated nodal stress from 58.34 to 26.53 MPa, while maximum movement increases from 0.090 to 0.164 mm. Ideal restraint peaks remain mesh sensitive. These results do not establish flight strength or minimum thickness.

Step 4: return detailed performance to the mission model

The mission model uses the CFD flow map and volume-weighted FEM resistance. A steady quadratic pump/loop pressure balance includes local channel losses plus an assumed remainder-loop resistance. The improved design uses a 16 m² radiator, approximately 2 L/min avionics flow and lower interface resistance, with radiator capacitance scaled with area. Pump energy rises from 0.44 to 0.75 kWh/day. The no-phase-change cold sensitivity calculates 17.68 versus 0 kWh heater energy for fixed versus bypass coupling. The fixed case drives bulk coolant to −8.97 °C, outside liquid-water model validity, so that value cannot size a real water-loop heater. The bypass radiator metal reaches −34.90 °C while bulk coolant stays above 14 °C. External fluid temperatures are not resolved, and radiator freeze protection or a separate external circuit remains an open design gate. Backup heaters remain necessary.

Step 5: challenge the design

With a 50% flow-loss fault, payload reaches 40.65 °C. A low-flow indication and a 20-second filtered temperature sensor enable latched 15% payload-power shedding, holding the peak below 40 °C. A separate +10% equipment-power sensitivity reaches 42.04 °C payload, so uncertainty margin remains unresolved. Unoccupied 5 °C startup uses zero crew heat and 20-second sensor lag; the cabin does not attain its occupied temperature band, requiring a crew-entry warm-up gate.

Step 6: prepare TVAC verification

TVAC means thermal vacuum testing. The package includes a powered-article model, proposed chamber/fixture boundaries, sensor plan, hot/cold pretest predictions, functional checks and a correlation approach using a withheld test case. These are test preparations and simulated predictions. No physical TVAC testing, actual correlation or flight qualification has been performed.

Numerical checks: 768 to 2,592 solid elements changes the improved peak by 0.074 K. Channel meshes increase from 5,760 to 46,080 cells at both 1 and 2 L/min. Mean-wall shifts are 0.280 and 0.212 K, with pressure shifts around 4.5–5%. The mission 10-to-5-second step comparison changes temperatures by less than 0.00005 K. These checks are separate from physical validation.

03 / Inputs before results

What is known, and what we assumed

Coupling boundary: the orbital model, 80 W solid panel and 2 W air-bay model are separate first-round studies. Their loads, temperatures and fields have not yet been reconciled into one common vehicle heat budget. The solid temperature field does feed its own thermoelastic solve.

Public vehicle anchors

Vast describes a 10.1 m by 4.4 m station, four crew members, 80 m³ pressurized volume and 45 m³ habitable volume, with solar power, avionics cold plates, a thermal pump tray, batteries and life support.

How to explain this analysis
  1. Define dissipation and operating modes independently from solar-array rating.
  2. Route heat through component interfaces to cold plates and coolant.
  3. Reject heat through a radiator with absorbed environmental flux and emitted radiation.
  4. Carry thermal inertia and electrical energy through sunlight and eclipse.
  5. Compare the same mission with one declared design change, then check numerical energy closure.

05 / Component thermal and thermoelastic response

Heat spreading and mounting restraint

The local model resolves solid temperature gradients and maps the temperature field into a linear-elastic mount analysis. It uses separate, simplified geometry and assumed material properties. It does not establish the station’s pressure-vessel strength, launch loads or fatigue life.

Thermal-to-structural workflow and next checks
  1. Assign a component heat load, cooling boundary and contact resistance.
  2. Solve the 3D solid conduction model and inspect mesh sensitivity.
  3. Apply thermal strain relative to a stated reference temperature.
  4. Compare declared mounting restraints and examine displacement and stress away from constraint singularities.
  5. Resolve the real bracket, fasteners, contact, preload and allowable stresses before any hardware decision.

06 / Pressurized internal-flow CFD

Cabin airflow is a different cooling problem

Forced ventilation carries heat inside the pressurized habitat. The representative bay uses microgravity with no buoyancy. Outside the vehicle, orbital vacuum heat rejection requires radiation and conduction modeling.

Baseline mesh comparison

What this CFD does and does not show

Velocity and pressure come from the declared OpenFOAM mesh and boundary conditions. Any air-temperature field is a separately identified energy solution. The local-domain result is not a validated full-cabin comfort, contaminant-removal or life-support design. Inlet placement, actual obstructions, fan curves, acoustic constraints and cabin pressure require further definition.

What follows this first round

Measured correlation

Characterize heat loads, contact conductance, coating optical properties and coolant/fan behavior. Define thermal-balance and thermal-cycle test cases, calibrated sensor locations, uncertainty and independent validation runs. TVAC applies to vacuum-exposed hardware; a pressurized cabin needs its own controlled air and coolant test boundaries.

Design refinement

Investigate radiator orientation and view factors, coating degradation, pump-out and heater faults, local board detail, real fastener/contact behavior, and fan/inlet placement. These remain follow-on tasks rather than completed or validated results.
