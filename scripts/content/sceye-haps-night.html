01 / Define the problem before optimizing

What did we assume?

Sceye publicly describes onboard computers, sensors, solar/battery power and under-platform payloads. Its design partner describes propulsion and embedded-system involvement. A telecom antenna is also public. The cited sources do not disclose an avionics board BOM, RF heat map, motor loss map or insulation stack, so those numerical details stay explicit assumptions.

Insulation is a heat path, not a color.

Stage02 used an effective battery case-to-jacket conductance of2W/K, without selecting an insulation product or thickness. For scale, k=0.020 W/mK over 5 m² needs 50 mm to give2W/K even before seam and cable leaks. An 80 mm layer would give 1.25W/K; adding an assumed 0.75W/K bypass returns 2 W/K. Neither example closes insulation mass or proves installation feasibility.

A reflective outer layer, foam/aerogel blanket and low-conductivity mounts are candidates. Their installed conductivity must be measured versus temperature, pressure, compression and moisture.20km air at about5.5kPa is not vacuum; vacuum-MLI properties cannot replace gas conduction and thin-air convection.

Targets, limits and margins are different.

Heater control targets are3°C avionics tray,8°C battery case,0°C payload plate and−5°C motor housing. They are chosen control settings, not component survival or operating limits. The processor, RF source and motor winding can differ substantially from their heater sensors.

The motor housing is a bearing-temperature proxy only. Battery charging logic is illustrative and is not a qualified lithium-sulfur or lithium-ion charging limit. Real selections require supplier data and altitude/temperature testing.

02 / Controlled design changes

Why each change affects temperature and energy

  1. Reproduce the reference. Same24 thermal masses, surfaces, electrical loads, environment, heater gains and capacities. With switch control disabled, the new source reproduces Stage02 within0.001K on the common final-day grid.
  2. Increase heater capacity alone. Raise avionics/payload/motor caps from 100/120/100W to300/360/300W. More electrical heat raises cold temperatures, but the continuously connected radiator still loses heat.
  3. Reduce cold radiator coupling. Replace each fixed strap by an ideal temperature-responsive thermal switch. At low base temperature it conducts10% of its open value; coupling rises smoothly to 100% over15K. Avionics opens fully at 20°C and payload at 17°C. Radiator convection, sky/Earth radiation and hull radiation remain active.
  4. Handle the motor separately. Keep its original exposed surface model and raise only its heater cap to 300 W. This combined case avoids saturation without pretending that the avionics switch also insulates the motor.
  5. Check the trade-offs. Compare1%,10%,25% residual conductance, colder air, direct solar exposure, doubled RF load, battery insulation and a switch stuck at low conductance all day. Lower energy alone does not demonstrate adequate hot-case protection.

These are ideal averaged proportional heaters and continuously modulated conductances. No specific thermal-switch product, actuator dynamics, controller power, added mass or reliability model is selected. Proportional control retains a finite temperature error: the combined motor housing still reaches about −10.6°C against its chosen −5°C target despite spare heater capacity. A tuned integral/hysteretic controller and verified component limits are a next control-design step.

04 / Representative electronics ·1D mission boundaries to3D conduction

A cool equipment node can hide a hot package.

The public electronics categories support studying processing, sensing, power conversion and RF heat paths. The first spatial example is a proposed120×80mm processing board with four generic bulk packages. Its40:20:15:10W split partitions the existing85W daytime processor aggregate; it does not add electrical load to the mission. No actual company circuit board or part number is implied.

What is solved?

Steady three-dimensional anisotropic conduction:∇·(k∇T)+q=0. Conforming8-node brick finite elements use2×2×2 Gauss integration. The bottom pad face is held at the native mission tray temperature; all other external faces are adiabatic. Uniform volumetric generation is applied inside the four package blocks.

PCB conductivity is assumed(20,20,0.69)W/mK. The0.69 reference comes from a public RF laminate datasheet, not an actual avionics stack. Package conductivity3W/mK and full-area pad3W/mK are screening assumptions. Improved effective through-plane conductivity5W/mK represents a possible via/spreader path without explicitly resolving vias.

The mission transfers boundary temperature and power to the spatial model. No PCB feedback is applied to the accepted mission network. The dynamic mission heat capacity remains an equipment aggregate; a frozen steady PCB solve cannot reproduce its transient storage.

05 / What the evidence now supports

Numerical checks, and the next engineering gates

Checks completed

  • Native OpenModelica compilation, conservative connections and original reference reproduction.
  • Every case: finite output, native and independently reconstructed energy accounting, SOC bounds and repeated-day settling.
  • Reference and combined candidate:30s/1e−8 versus60s/1e−7 numerical refinement.
  • 3D FEM analytical slab check, reaction/source balance and three mesh levels. Medium→fine peak change0.096% nominal; below0.14% in all three constructions.
  • Native FreeCAD reopening and STEP re-import: six valid solids.

These checks establish consistency of the implemented assumptions. They do not establish physical accuracy.

Parameters investigated in this stage

  • Radiator cold-state conductance1%,10%,25%:1% saves almost no additional energy versus10% in this profile.
  • Battery jacketG2→1W/K reduces combined heater demand 2.420→1.648kWh/day, before any added insulation mass.
  • Air temperature, solar exposure, payload power and switch fault expose separate cold/hot drivers.
  • PCB through-plane path and thermal-pad condition strongly affect local hot spots under the same mission boundary.
Further thermal challenges and focused analyses
  1. Controller design: remove proportional offset, implement hysteresis/integral action, sensor location/error, minimum-on/off times, actuator delay and safe fault states. First establish actual equipment limits; this stage does not claim target compliance.
  2. Electronics qualification: replace generic blocks with selected CPU/FPGA, DC–DC, MPPT and RF package models; resolve copper/via heat paths and interfaces. Measure board cold-plate heat flow and temperatures at altitude, then correlate the3D model before reducing it to mission conductances.
  3. Environment: bound season, latitude, cloud/albedo, low-density convection, ascent/descent and cold-soak startup. Use measured installed optics and geometry-derived view factors. The existing solar/yaw sensitivities are not a flight simulation.
  4. Insulation and mass: include seams, harnesses, fasteners and compressed blankets; trade installedk(T,p), thickness and mass. Reclose battery capacity, solar area, energy reserve and buoyancy after adding equipment.
  5. Battery and propulsion: selected-cell loss/charge maps, gradients and BMS limits; motor winding/magnet/ESC/bearing temperatures with altitude losses and cold-start loads. Thermal runaway/venting and pressure-vessel analyses require separate models.
  6. Payload and structure: RF efficiency/load shedding, antenna distortion and interface gradients; optical payload alignment if selected; low-pressure electrical insulation/partial discharge and UV/weather protection.
  7. Test and correlation: component interface tests, thermal-vacuum/altitude distinction, temperature cycling, chamber solar/IR balance, thermocouple uncertainty and separate calibration/holdout missions. No physical tests have been performed for this study.

Primary public sources and the limits of each source
  • S1 · Sceye platform: public subsystem categories

    Onboard computers, sensors, battery power, solar supply and downward-looking mission payloads. Boundary: Board vendors, package powers, insulation stack, interfaces, controller setpoints or this model topology.

  • S2 · Mobius: propulsion and embedded-system design involvement

    Public design-partner relationship for propulsion and embedded systems. Boundary: Motor, ESC or avionics specifications; no public BOM recovered.

  • S3 · SceyeCELL public telecom payload announcement

    A custom stratospheric telecommunications antenna is a public payload category. Boundary: RF device identities, heat map, circuit layout or RF conversion efficiency used here.

  • S4 · NASA thermal-control survey: heat switches and heat paths

    Thermal switches vary conductive coupling; literature includes conductance ratios on the order of10:1. Boundary: A selected switch for this aircraft, our20km convection coefficients, control law, mass or flight readiness.

  • S5 · NASA layered composite insulation for nonvacuum exposure

    Aerogel blanket composite concepts are relevant nonvacuum insulation candidates. Boundary: Our numerical k, thickness, pressure dependence, mass or a Sceye insulation selection.

  • S6 · NASA MLI pressure-dependence research

    Gas conduction and pressure history affect MLI performance. Boundary: Treating20km as vacuum or using vacuum-MLI effective emissivity as a complete insulation model.

  • S7 · Rogers RO4000 laminate data sheet

    RO4350B typical bulk thermal conductivity0.69W/mK is a public material reference. Boundary: Sceye material choice, copper stack, anisotropic effective conductivity, package/junction thermal resistance or valid constant properties over our whole range.

  • S8 · US Standard Atmosphere1976

    Nominal standard-atmosphere reference; used20km values are documented. Boundary: Weather envelope, convection validation or actual mission telemetry.
