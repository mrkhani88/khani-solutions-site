Step 1 of 6

01 / Begin with the purpose

Keep the service available, through day and night.

The primary study is a representative telecom and onboard-computing configuration. Observation equipment is treated as an alternative payload rather than assumed to fly alongside every telecom installation.

Engineering definition

The mission basis

Sceye’s 2026 ST1 report describes telecom operations at 16.5–17 km, with onboard server and mobile-network functions. We retain the earlier 20 km reference and explicitly compare an added lower-altitude mission condition.

  • Environment: solar input, Earth/sky radiation, air temperature, altitude and prescribed wind.
  • Service: electronics temperatures, availability, cold-start permissions and shared electrical demand.
  • Endurance: daytime charging, nighttime battery reserve and heater energy.

Follow the energy

  1. Prescribed environment and operating modes generate electrical and thermal loads.
  2. Finite conductances, radiation and convection determine component temperatures.
  3. Heaters and conversion losses feed back into battery demand.
  4. Operating permissions connect component temperature to modeled service availability.

Numerical checks and physical limits

Review energy closure, time-step sensitivity and repeated-day behavior. These verify the implementation. They do not establish that the assumed heat paths or component data match flight hardware.

04 / Make the design buildable and testable

A defined article, interfaces and measurement plan.

The test-ready package specifies a representative electronics assembly, candidate sensors, electrical measurements, environmental sequence and acceptance logic. Hardware fabrication and physical test execution remain future work.

Physical test planned

What the plan defines

  1. Board, mounting plate, interface stack and controlled heat sources.
  2. Sensor locations, synchronized voltage/current data and boundary-condition measurements.
  3. Room-pressure characterization, repeated assembly and independent power cycles.
  4. Altitude and radiation conditions requiring appropriate chamber equipment.
  5. Uncertainty, configuration identity, pass/fail rules and review records.

A room-pressure experiment can characterize selected paths and capacities. It cannot establish stratospheric convection or flight qualification.

Resolve integration before the test

Review fasteners, surface finish, interface compression, harness heat leaks, pressure boundaries and sensor installation. The interface record makes each assumption a reviewable engineering item.

Interview takeaway

“I turn a numerical design into an instrumented, controlled experiment with explicit acceptance and uncertainty.”

05 / Separate fitting from prediction

Calibrate once. Predict a held-out sequence.

A synthetic exercise demonstrates parameter fitting and a separate held-out prediction. The thermal topology is related but the generator includes a selected nonlinear discrepancy, sensor bias and noise.

Physical test planned

The correlation workflow

  1. Declare parameter bounds and a residual metric before fitting.
  2. Fit conductances and capacities using the calibration sequence only.
  3. Keep the fitted parameters fixed for a different power/boundary sequence.
  4. Report signed residuals, RMSE and maximum absolute error.
  5. Replace synthetic observations with calibrated physical measurements before validation claims.

The synthetic generator and fitted model share topology. A successful workflow check is not independent evidence of physical accuracy.

What the synthetic exercise establishes

It checks the correlation workflow, parameter recovery and reporting. It does not provide real measurement evidence or independent physical validation.

06 / Close the review with risks and evidence

Define what can operate—and what remains open.

The readiness package links requirements to analysis, planned tests, design decisions and residual risks. Fault cases examine a degraded thermal path and selected control/power disruptions.

Readiness limits explicit

Readiness questions

  • Can electronics start at their current temperatures?
  • What happens when a heater or thermal interface fails?
  • Which loads should be reduced when reserve is insufficient?
  • What evidence is needed before release to hardware test?

Study-defined operating limits illustrate the method; hardware-selected limits, mission data, environmental qualification and flight readiness remain unresolved.

Controlled closeout

Inspect the risk register, verification matrix, standards applicability and decision log. No claim of certified compliance or completed qualification is made.

Interview takeaway

“I can show what is supported, what can fail, and the evidence needed for the next release decision.”

Modeled explicitly

Still simplified

Prescribed wind/flight demand, generic electrical loss maps, mean gas temperature, effective contacts and enclosure convection. A detailed OEM bill of materials is unavailable.

Further hardware work

Selected part limits, measured losses and interfaces, chemistry-specific battery data, environmental tests, physical correlation and operational qualification.
