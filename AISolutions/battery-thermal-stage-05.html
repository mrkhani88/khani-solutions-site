Why the temperatures change, step by step

1 · Preserve the thermal-pad reference

The pad-only case exactly reproduces Stage 04: 2 W in the cell, 1.75 W across seven packages, 25 °C initial and ambient temperature, sources on for 600 s and off until 1,800 s. The exterior display, back glass and frame retain the same 8 W/(m²·K) effective loss coefficient. The saved baseline field is checked byte for byte against Stage 04.

2 · Install a sheet inside the same enclosure

The assumed graphite sheet is 30 × 50 × 0.10 mm, over x = 38–68 mm and y = 30–80 mm. A 0.025 mm adhesive bonds it to the inner back glass. To fit both layers, the installed RF pad changes from 2.2 to 2.075 mm. The outer cover, frame and external cooling area stay fixed.

This is an assembly change that includes a thinner pad and added interfaces. It is therefore accompanied by a conductivity control to isolate lateral spreading. CAD checks find 67 valid solids without overlap, 80 mm² of pad-to-graphite contact and 1,500 mm² at each bonded-sheet interface.

3 · Model anisotropy and the real thermal paths

Gx = kx · t · Δy/Δx   ·   Rz = L/(kzA)   ·   Q̇ = ΔT/R

The graphite assumption is kx = ky = 800 W/(m·K) and kz = 5 W/(m·K). It conducts much more readily along the sheet than through its thickness. Its sheet conductance, kxyt = 0.08 W/K per square, is 100 times that of the modeled 0.8 mm glass layer, whose value is 0.0008 W/K per square.

Graphite density is assumed to be 1,800 kg/m³ and specific heat 700 J/(kg·K). The adhesive uses k = 0.2 W/(m·K), density 1,200 kg/m³ and specific heat 1,500 J/(kg·K), with 0.0001 m²·K/W at each bonded interface. The pad-to-graphite interface retains the pad’s 0.0002 m²·K/W contact assumption. Half-layer resistances are included.

Both new layers have their own temperature fields and finite heat capacity. Their masses are 0.270 g and 0.045 g. Removing 0.125 mm of pad offsets 0.022 g, so the net added mass is 0.293 g and net added capacity is 0.2345 J/K. Internal air/radiation paths terminate on graphite where it covers a surface; the corresponding old direct-to-glass paths are removed. There is no new sink to ambient.

For background, Panasonic documents high X–Y conductivity for PGS graphite sheets. The values above form an independent illustrative material set; they are not taken as the specifications of a selected Panasonic grade.

4 · Separate lateral spreading from the other stack changes

The diagnostic control keeps the new graphite/adhesive geometry, mass, contacts and through-thickness conductivity. It changes only the sheet’s in-plane conductivity from 800 to 5 W/(m·K). It represents a numerical control, not another commercial material.

5 · Read the peak and the warm area together

With the pad alone, heat enters a small back-cover region and the glass spreads it weakly. Graphite carries heat outward before it crosses the adhesive into the cover. The cover maximum falls by 11.06 K, while its mean changes only from 36.64 to 36.68 °C.

The cover maximum shifts from the control-volume center at (55, 55) mm over the pad to (35, 95) mm, outside the sheet and over the cell’s prescribed hot-source patch. The RF-pad region is no longer the location of the global cover maximum. The area above 40 °C grows even as the hot spot weakens. These thresholds are visualization diagnostics, not touch-temperature or safety limits. Areas are calculated from finite-volume cell-center values, with entire control-volume areas counted at each threshold; they are not interpolated contour-boundary measurements.

The RF board’s maximum also falls, from 61.25 to 55.02 °C. At 600 s, pad-to-graphite heat flow is 0.1760 W; the pad-only reference sends 0.0903 W directly from pad to glass. Across the full sheet footprint, adhesive-to-cover flow is 0.2914 W, which includes heat from several neighboring parts and must not be interpreted as pad heat alone.

The cell mean changes from 40.94 to 41.02 °C. The sheet couples to a small cell-edge area through its internal gap, so reducing the RF or cover peak does not guarantee that every component cools. That small modeled difference is not a demonstrated hardware margin.

All three cases receive 2,250 J by 600 s. Exterior losses are 835.87 J for pad only, 839.96 J for graphite and 835.21 J for the control. The large peak reduction accompanies a small change in total heat rejection at this time: redistribution over the cover is the dominant effect.

6 · Verify the result and retain the limits

The pad reference has 9,710 thermal nodes; the new stack has 10,460, including 375 graphite and 375 adhesive control volumes. The finer stack has 40,236 nodes. All cases use the same conservative link equations and a 2 s implicit time step; 1 s runs test time-step sensitivity.

  • Reference preservation: Stage 04 pad-only temperatures match byte for byte. Source vectors and exterior sinks are unchanged; original capacities change only in the shortened pad.
  • Control isolation: the new-stack and control masses, sources, contacts and all other conductances are checked equal; only graphite’s lateral links change.
  • Energy balance: largest residual across configurations is 1.00e-09 J.
  • Source superposition: isolated-source rises sum to the combined rise within 1.56e-13 K before browser rounding.
  • Spatial refinement: largest component-maximum difference across new-stack/control checks at 600 s is 0.4413 K.
  • Time refinement: largest component-maximum difference at 600 s is 0.00793 K.
  • Effect refinement: cover-maximum reduction is 11.06 K on the working grid and 11.49 K on the finer grid; the predicted effect changes by 0.44 K.

These checks assess numerical consistency, not physical accuracy. Each planar control volume has one unknown through thickness; vertical paths enter as resistances. Internal flow, nonlinear radiation, junction/skin gradients, graphite bending, compression, electrical isolation and manufacturing details remain outside the model. Material and contact measurements plus independent thermal tests are needed for physical validation.
