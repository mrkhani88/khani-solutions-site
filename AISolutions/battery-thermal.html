We start with the energy balance for a pouch-cell equivalent. A prescribed heat source warms the cell; heat transfer to the surroundings cools it. Every additional stage will answer one new question.

All material and boundary-condition values are teaching assumptions. This is a solved, numerically verified model; it has no experimental validation and predicts no Apple or Wisk product performance.

Heat input: 1.0 W

The model in one equation

C · dT/dt = Q̇in − G · (T − Tambient)

C is the cell's thermal capacity in J/K. G is the effective conductance to ambient in W/K. The temperature rises when heat input exceeds the heat transferred out.

Heating and cooling result

With these inputs, the average temperature reaches 37.33 °C at 10 minutes and falls to 26.48 °C at 30 minutes. The temperature stays continuous when the heat source switches off; its slope changes.

Energy is conserved

The source supplies 600 J in total. At each time, that energy is split between energy stored above the initial temperature and cumulative heat transferred to the surroundings. The maximum computed balance residual is 1.02e-12 J. Numerical and analytical temperatures agree within 3.43e-10 K; this verifies the implementation for these assumptions.

What to learn before adding complexity

  1. At the start, the cell is at ambient, so heat loss is zero and the initial slope is Q̇in/C.
  2. As the cell warms, heat loss grows. Its temperature therefore rises more slowly.
  3. If heating continued indefinitely, the temperature rise would approach Q̇in/G. It does not reach that value during this finite pulse.
  4. After heat-off, the cell still contains stored energy. It cools gradually with a time constant τ = C/G.
  5. A close analytical match checks the numerical implementation. Measurements are still needed to validate the physical assumptions.
