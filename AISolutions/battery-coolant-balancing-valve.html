Designing a battery coolant balancing valve in FreeCAD

From 6.90 L/min to a 2.27 mm estimated needle lift in one of six parallel coolant branches for an electric-aircraft battery pack.

Conceptual sizing · The calculated lift is a starting estimate for flow testing.

I designed this manual balancing valve for one of six parallel coolant branches in an electric-aircraft battery pack concept. The 42.5-second video shows the valve assembly and installation concept on the Module 2 branch, a schematic cutaway of the flow path, and a 15-second FreeCAD build walkthrough.

See the six-branch coolant distribution design

Set the flow requirement

One branch needs 6.90 L/min of 60% propylene glycol at 30°C, with 6.0 kPa allocated to the valve. Using a specific gravity of 1.043, the target flow coefficient is Kᵥ = 0.414 × √(1.043 ÷ 0.060) = 1.73 m³/h.

Lay out the valve body

The Ø14 mm inlet and outlet match the branch bore. Coolant enters from the bottom and exits through a 90° side outlet. The Ø36 mm body leaves 7 mm of nominal wall around the Ø22 mm chamber, before accounting for port intersections and other local features.

Size the seat and needle

Using Q = CᴅA√(2Δp/ρ), an assumed discharge coefficient of 0.62, and 6.0 kPa across the seat, I calculated a required opening of 54.7 mm². A Ø12 mm seat and 45° needle cone turn axial motion into a ring-shaped flow area. Solving for that area gives 2.27 mm of starting lift.

Design the stem, handwheel, and stops

The handwheel moves a Ø8 mm stem and needle together. The dry guide sets the 0.50 mm lower stop, and a collar limits lift to 3.00 mm. With a proposed 1 mm/rev adjuster, the starting setting is 1.77 turns above the lower stop.

Design the removable bonnet and seals

The Ø44 mm bonnet provides access to the needle and supports the stem seal. An Ø29 mm face gasket seals the stationary body joint. A separate 8 × 2.2 mm O-ring envelope represents the seal around the moving stem.

Retain the bonnet

Four M4 screw envelopes sit on a Ø34 mm bolt circle. The model has Ø4.4 mm bonnet clearance holes and Ø3.3 mm blind tap-drill envelopes in the body.

Check the lift estimate

Reserving 0.5 kPa for the ports and turn raised the estimated lift from 2.27 to 2.38 mm, still within the modeled travel. FreeCAD reported zero invalid shapes across all ten assembly parts. The next validation step is to measure flow versus lift with PG60 at nominal and cold conditions, then verify leakage and pressure integrity separately.
