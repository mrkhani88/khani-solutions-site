Rivian R1T vs. Tesla Model S P85

Silent FreeCAD walkthrough · Independent reconstruction using public references and illustrative dimensions.

Where should you pull heat out of a cylindrical battery cell? In this FreeCAD walkthrough, I worked through two approaches from a blank sketch. The Rivian-style plate collects heat through the cell ends. The Tesla-style ribbon collects it through the cell sidewalls.

Rivian R1T Quad · 141 kWh in FEV’s benchmark

One flat liquid cold plate sits between two cell stacks, so both faces of the plate collect heat. This gives both stacks a shared cooling surface and preserves a thin module layout. Rivian’s patent shows counterflow passages and fins placed selectively downstream to improve temperature uniformity as the coolant warms. The manufacturing challenges are keeping both contact faces flat, sealing the plate, and maintaining good thermal contact across the stacks. FEV battery specifications · FEV module layout · Rivian patent

Tesla Model S P85 · 85 kWh original rating

A flattened coolant ribbon winds between staggered rows of 18650 cells. The scallops follow the cylindrical cans, increasing the sidewall contact area while fitting into the gaps between rows. The challenge is holding that fit across many cells while bending the tube without constricting its internal passages. The module loops receive coolant in parallel, so the manifolds must distribute flow evenly. Tesla specifications · NHTSA cooling-system description · Tesla ribbon patent application

The thermal and hydraulic tradeoff

A wider flow area can lower coolant velocity in the flat plate, while shallow passages, ports, turns, and fins can still contribute significant pressure loss. In the ribbon, small passages and long runs can raise pressure drop. Parallel module feeds split the pack flow among the branches. At a given flow rate, lower pressure drop reduces hydraulic pump power. To compare cooling performance, I’d match the heat load, coolant, and inlet temperature, then measure peak cell temperature and cell-to-cell temperature spread at equal pump power. How AI assisted the build

I used AI to interpret public drawings, establish reference dimensions, plan the feature order, and check the fit and coolant connections. The video shows the actual FreeCAD operations from clean sheet to assembly, including the Rivian channel pocket and hollow ports, the Tesla ribbon’s four passages cut one at a time, the repeated R9.25 mm scallops, and the two staggered rows patterned from a single Ø18 mm cell.
