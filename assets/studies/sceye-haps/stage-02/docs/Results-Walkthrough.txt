READ THE STAGE 02 RESULTS, STEP BY STEP

1. Start with the reference case and the Environment plot. Local solar time0 h is
midnight,6–18 h is equinox daylight. Relative wind and body attitude are prescribed.
The platform performs station keeping; this is not a launch-to-landing trajectory.

2. Watch electrical power. Solar serves load and recharges the battery. When SOC
approaches92%, charging tapers and surplus is curtailed. Panel electrical export,
MPPT loss and distribution loss are placed in different thermal nodes so energy
is not counted twice. Nighttime comes from the battery; its efficiency losses
become heat. All powers and capacities are illustrative assumptions.

3. Select each subsystem in the native connection map. Open individual components
to compare core/junction, mounting tray and radiator temperatures. A cold radiator
does not establish a cold electronic junction, and a warm average does not resolve
a cell hot spot. The color map represents lumped temperatures, not a spatial field.

4. Compare heater power with temperature. Reference heating totals about5.26 kWh
per displayed day, included within about63.40 kWh of DC-bus demand. Avionics,
payload and motor heaters reach their assumed caps during cold periods. The
battery core stays roughly12.47–22.92 C, with delayed response from its large heat
capacity and charge/discharge losses. Processor and winding temperatures can fall
below0 C even with heating. These are design findings, not certified limits.

5. Expose the radiators to direct sun. The same heat-rejection area absorbs more
solar power; component temperatures rise while some heater demand falls. Lower
heater energy alone does not make a better thermal design.

6. Double only the payload input. The RF block reaches about167 C in this assumed
constant-loss model, and total DC demand rises to about72.55 kWh/day. Shared
conversion, battery and mounting paths also respond. The model does not disable
a failed electronic part; this high temperature is a conditional diagnostic and
requires redesign/selected-part review, not a claim that the mission operates.

7. Reduce only radiator strap conductance. The temperature drop across each strap
increases for a given heat flow, raising electronics temperatures while reducing
cold-period heater use. Similar total energy can conceal a severe local hot spot.

8. Read the verification evidence. All eight native runs finish, seven displayed
cases settle to repeatable last cycles within0.02 K for every node, and the
reference numerical refinement changes sampled node temperatures by less than
0.0005 K. Independent quadrature closes within0.1% of integrated eight-day DC load
energy; exact values and separate native residuals are in numerical-audit.json.
These are numerical checks, not experimental validation or accurate significant
figures. Hardware and environment assumptions dominate the present uncertainty.

9. Next: obtain defensible component loss/limit/property ranges, compare controlled
night isolation with higher heater ratings, improve radiator placement/shielding,
and run targeted spatial thermal models. Then use real low-pressure-air and
radiative tests for calibration and independent holdout correlation.
