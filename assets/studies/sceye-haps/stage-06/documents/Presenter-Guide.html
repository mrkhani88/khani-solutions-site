A 10–15 minute thermal-engineering walkthrough

Present one connected engineering story: mission needs, temperature and energy predictions, component-level design, test planning, correlation method, and fault/readiness decisions.

State this at the start: This is my independent engineering study using public information and declared assumptions. I have simulation evidence and a prepared test package; no Sceye internal hardware, physical tests or qualification results are claimed.

1 · Mission and requirements

Question: What service must remain available, and what inputs are known?

Evidence: Publicly described telecom and edge-computing mission; chosen thermal/service/energy objectives; source/assumption register.

Decision: Use a representative telecom/server configuration. Add a17km case alongside inherited20km; keep observation payload as an alternative.

2 · Integrated mission model

Question: How does a component change affect the complete mission?

Evidence: Connected temperatures, heating, electrical/RF heat partition, workload/service output and SOC; compare inherited and detailed architecture.

Decision: Judge temperature, service and energy together. Throttling or an inhibit must be visible rather than counted as full service.

Limitation: Prescribed wind/environment and generic battery are not an actual flight replay.

3 · Spatial detail and design trade

Question: Can the physical heat path support the resistance assumed in1D?

Evidence: Representative board/plate geometry, spatial hot spots and mesh audit; compare interface and nighttime-path changes, then return a reduced path to the mission.

Decision: Improve local conduction and control avoidable nighttime leakage separately; retain rejected or failing candidates.

Limitation: Bulk case temperatures are not selected-part junction temperatures; material/contact values need measurement.

4 · Test-ready assembly

Question: What experiment would establish that this model predicts hardware?

Evidence: Candidate sensors, electrical metrology, configuration record, room/chamber/altitude/radiation ladder, repeat assembly and raw CSV schema.

Decision: Separate gas/convection and shroud/radiation effects. Choose real limits and independent trip before testing.

Limitation: Article not assembled; instruments and chamber runs not executed.

5 · Correlation and prediction

Question: Can we predict a condition that was not used to fit parameters?

Evidence: Generated calibration and held-out cases; frozen parameters, residual plots and uncertainty method.

Decision: Fit few identifiable parameters and evaluate held-out errors without retuning.

Limitation: This example uses synthetic observations. It is a method demonstration, not a physical validation or calibration of the five-node assembly.

Question: What fails, how do we detect it, and what evidence would release the design?
