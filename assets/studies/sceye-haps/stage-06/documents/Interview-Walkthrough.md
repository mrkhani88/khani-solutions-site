# Interview walkthrough and closeout boundary

Open the main guided page. The six steps each link the relevant job duties, assumptions, results and downloads. A useful short walkthrough is:

1. **Mission and requirements:** explain persistent telecom/edge-computing service and distinguish public vehicle facts from chosen thermal inputs.
2. **Integrated mission model:** follow electrical power into heat, component temperatures, heater demand, service availability and battery reserve.
3. **Component detail and trade:** inspect the representative PCB/plate temperature map, mesh checks and the effect of revised interfaces/nighttime paths on mission results.
4. **Test-ready assembly:** inspect the sensor map, configuration checklist, environment ladder, raw-data template and test matrix. State that hardware tests have not been executed.
5. **Correlation workflow:** show calibration versus held-out generated data, frozen parameters and residuals. State that synthetic data demonstrate method, not measured accuracy.
6. **Faults and readiness:** review changed sensors, lost heater, degraded contact and resulting actions/risks. Finish with the job-requirement matrix and its explicit physical-evidence gaps.

Use the same evidence for several requirements where appropriate: a cold-start case demonstrates transient analysis, controller design, requirements evaluation and an identified risk. Multiple links do not mean independent validation.

The portfolio is a complete simulation-and-planning package at its declared scope. It is not a recovered OEM model, a verified flight design, a physical qualification campaign or evidence that the author worked on Sceye hardware. Physical build, laboratory execution, real data correlation and organizational/authority approvals remain real-world activities with defined closure criteria.

Suggested review question: “Which assumption would you replace first if hardware became available?” Answer with a source-backed list: equipment power/loss maps, allowable temperatures and service criteria, interface conductance, chamber/flight environmental histories, mounted-sensor response and usable battery maps. Do not guess confidential vehicle internals.
