THERMAL VERIFICATION AND INTEGRATION PLAN / NOT EXECUTED

REQUIREMENTS FIRST
Obtain component operational, non-operational, startup, charge and gradient/rate
limits. Specify mission latitude/date/duration, wind/attitude, power modes,
pressure/venting, mounting and end-of-life properties. Identify the applicable
aviation/program verification basis with the hardware owner. NASA/RTCA/ECSS
references here guide planning; they do not establish applicability or compliance.

PROPOSED TEST MATRIX
A. Ground thermal cycle: modes OFF/standby/peak, controlled heat loads, representative
   mounts, measured temperatures and power; evaluate hot ground cooling.
B. Altitude-air balance: mission-equivalent low pressure with defined gas, air
   temperature, relative flow and radiation boundaries; do not substitute vacuum
   without accounting for the convection difference.
C. Radiative thermal balance: instrument panel/baseplate/strap interfaces under
   controlled incident IR/solar where needed; measure optical properties.
D. Thermal-vacuum: isolate radiative/conductive paths and evaluate hardware only
   where required by the selected program; not by itself an altitude-air validation.
E. Battery temperature/charge/discharge: selected cell supplier's approved envelope,
   calibrated power/heat data and independent protection, conducted in an equipped lab.
F. Motor/ESC: measured electrical input, shaft output and distributed losses across
   speed/load/altitude; instrument winding/case/bearing/controller/mount paths.
G. Integration fault cases: heater stuck-off/on, degraded contact, changed payload
   power, pressure/venting fault and sensor failures, within an approved test procedure.

INSTRUMENTATION
Calibrated contact temperatures at battery core/surface proxies, avionics/RF hot
spots, both sides of critical interfaces, radiator roots/tips, motor/controller
mounts and structural/optical references. Log pressure, gas/air temperature,
electrical power, heater state, battery SOC and the applied environment.
Do not equate an uncorrected IR camera image with temperature on reflective hulls.
Include sensor contact/radiation errors, calibration, sampling, synchronization
and repeatability in the uncertainty budget.

CORRELATION
Use selected calibration tests to identify a bounded small set of observable
parameters (for example contact resistance). Keep separate holdout pressure/power
conditions for prediction. Compare peak/minimum temperatures, time constants,
interface heat flows and heater Wh. Report errors alongside instrument uncertainty.
Check parameter identifiability; fitting one temperature cannot uniquely determine
all loss, emissivity and interface coefficients. Physical test data are unavailable
in Stage 01. No synthetic data are presented as measurements.

INTEGRATION RESPONSIBILITIES
Mechanical: assembly CAD, mounting preload/contact area, materials, venting and
           structural/optical gradient limits.
Electrical: measured dissipation by mode, RF export, heater/sensor circuitry,
            protection logic and low-pressure electrical concerns.
Systems: mission/environment envelopes, acceptable margins, operating rules,
         power/energy reserves and failure response.
Manufacturing: tolerances, surface finish, bondline thickness, insulation installation,
               wire routing, workmanship and inspection points.
Test/reliability: calibration, pressure/radiative boundary reproduction, safe fixtures,
                  repeatability and approved test acceptance criteria.

RELEASE GATES
Source/assumption review -> model verification -> design comparison -> independent
review -> approved hardware test -> correlation/holdout closure -> integration
verification -> program qualification/readiness review. Maintain a deviation/action
log with owners and dispositions. No program release authority exists in this study.
